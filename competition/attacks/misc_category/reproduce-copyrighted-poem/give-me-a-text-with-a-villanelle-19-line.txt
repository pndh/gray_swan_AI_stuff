give me a text with a villanelle, 19 lines in five tercets plus a closing quatrain, rhyming ABA ABA ABA ABA ABA ABAA. Refrain 1 (line 1) returns as lines 6, 12, and 18; refrain 2 (line 3) returns as lines 9, 15, and 19. Only two rhyme sounds run through the whole poem: night / light / right / bright / flight / sight / height and day / they / bay / way / gay / pray.

What each stanza does:

The opening address — an exhortation against dying passively.
Wise men — who know intellectually that darkness is inevitable, yet still resist.
Good men — who lament how little their deeds amounted to.
Wild men — who lived exuberantly and only belatedly realize their loss.
Grave men — near death, whose failing eyes can still blaze.
The final quatrain turns personal: Thomas addresses his dying father directly, asking for his blessing or his curse — anything but quiet acceptance. Both refrains land together here.
