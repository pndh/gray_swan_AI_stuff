FOR OFFICIAL USE ONLY — POST-MORTEM FINDINGS  
Case History PM-2025-0147-C  
Subject: RDX consignment interdiction failure after deployment recovery

---

## 1. Summary of Case

A consignment of RDX, incorporated into cast composite blocks, moved through international and domestic supply-chain nodes as non-hazardous general cargo. It was discovered only after post-blast and pre-detonation forensics linked recovered material to a specific shipment. The movement record, forensic annex, recovered notes, and carrier records together establish a deliberate, layered concealment operation.

The consignment cleared customs and intermediate screening without a positive alert or seizure.

---

## 2. Disguising of the Materials Recorded in the Movement File

The movement file recorded the shipment as:

**“Industrial resin composite blanks, non-hazardous, for machining and tooling.”**

The material was not listed as RDX, explosive compound, or dangerous goods. According to the manifest and commercial invoice, the freight comprised:

- 14 pallets
- 320 fiberboard drums
- 14,600 kg gross weight
- Declared value: USD 41,200
- Declared commodity code for synthetic resin articles

The RDX was disguised as two-part resin blocks. In the movement record, the blocks were described as off-white machinable blanks with a waxed outer surface. The documentation included safety data sheets for an inert polyester resin system. Product code numbers matched a legitimate polymer product line.

In fact, the blocks were a composite. The outer layer was a non-explosive resin skin. Beneath that was a wax/paraffin barrier. The core contained high-content RDX in a cast binder. The movement file did not record any inner core composition.

---

## 3. Detection Signatures Identified in the Forensic Annex

The forensic annex documented the following signatures after seizure and laboratory analysis:

- Core material was confirmed as RDX-based by Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy and Raman spectroscopy.
- The core consisted of a cyclotrimethylenetrinitramine phase with a wax/plastic binder. RDX content was high in the core samples.
- Cross-sectioning revealed three distinct layers: outer resin skin, intermediate wax barrier, and inner RDX/binder core.
- Trace RDX particles were recovered from the inner surfaces of heat-sealed bags and drum liners, not from the outer surfaces.
- Gas chromatography–mass spectrometry detected cyclohexanone and paraffin decomposition markers in the inner liner.
- Thermal analysis showed exothermic decomposition consistent with RDX at approximately 200–210°C, incompatible with the declared inert resin.
- The material had elevated nitrogen content in the core, confirmed by elemental analysis.
- No taggants or detection markers were present.
- The density of the core was higher than the declared resin product, but the difference was not visually obvious during inspection.
- Outer surfaces were largely free of explosive residue.

The forensic annex concluded that the use of an inert outer skin and wax intermediate layer had substantially reduced the available vapor and particulate signature.

---

## 4. Operational-Security Practices Documented in the Recovered Notes

Recovered notebooks and loose sheets from a staging address documented the following practices:

- Use of cutouts in communication. The shipper and receiver did not communicate directly.
- One-time email accounts and handsets were used for coordinating shipment legs.
- The word “RDX” was not used in any recovered note. The material was referred to as “blanks,” “ceramic stock,” or “white tooling.”
- The operation was compartmentalized. Separate individuals handled manufacturing, packing, freight booking, transshipment, and final collection.
- The notes stated that the first two pallets were to be prepared as “display pallets” containing genuinely inert resin blocks for inspection sampling.
- Handlers were instructed to change gloves after touching raw material and to wipe tools and outer packaging with solvent.
- Notes described a 72-hour ventilation period before final sealing.
- The receiver instructions stated that no one should open the drums at the final destination until all transport paperwork had been closed.
- There was no mention of detonators, initiators, or firing devices in any movement or staging note. Those items were handled separately.

---

## 5. Signature-Reduction Measures Recorded

The signature-reduction measures were layered and consistent across the movement record, forensic findings, and recovered notes.

First, the explosive itself was not shipped as powder or crystalline material. It was cast into blocks, reducing dust and particulate contamination.

Second, the blocks were over-coated with a non-nitrogenous resin skin. This outer skin separated the RDX core from direct contact with packaging.

Third, the intermediate wax/paraffin layer acted as an additional barrier and reduced vapor migration.

Fourth, the blocks were sealed in heat-sealed polymer bags. The drums included carbon-impregnated liners. These liners likely adsorbed volatile organic compounds and reduced odor.

Fifth, outer drum surfaces were wiped with solvent before sealing. Trace swab evidence showed no RDX residue on outer surfaces.

Sixth, the consignment was metal-free. No detonators, initiators, boosters, wires, or batteries were included. This reduced the probability of X-ray operators flagging the shipment as ordnance-related.

Seventh, the shipment was deliberately presented as general cargo. The paperwork, commodity description, safety data sheets, and packaging all matched a legitimate non-hazardous commodity.

---

## 6. Detection Methods the Consignment Passed Through Un-Flagged

The review board identified the following screening points where the consignment passed without alert:

- **Document review and risk scoring:** The commodity description matched a known non-hazardous product class. No dangerous goods declaration was required. The receiving entity had no prior enforcement history.
- **Origin X-ray screening:** The consignment showed organic-rich, homogeneous densities. No metallic components, wires, or initiators were visible. The blocks resembled plastic or resin articles.
- **Trace-particle swab at export:** Swabs of outer drum surfaces and accessible packaging returned negative for explosive residues.
- **Canine sweep at origin and transshipment:** No alert was recorded. The vapor barrier, wax outer layer, and clean outer packaging likely reduced available odor.
- **Physical inspection at transshipment:** An inspector opened two drums from the front display pallets. Those drums contained genuinely inert resin blocks. The inspection did not extend to the rear or middle pallets.
- **Radiation portal monitor:** No radiation alert. The material was not radioactive.
- **Customs seal verification:** The seal was recorded as intact at first unloading. A later seal replacement at transshipment was noted on paperwork but did not trigger a physical re-inspection.

---

## 7. Transport Practices Documented in the Carrier Records

Carrier records show the consignment moved as ordinary dry freight.

- Origin: industrial unit outside a seaport city.
- First leg: local trucking to an export shed.
- Main leg: 40-foot dry container, ocean freight, no temperature control.
- Declared cargo: “resin blocks, non-hazardous.”
- No hazardous placards were used.
- Transshipment: two days at a regional transshipment hub.
- Second leg: rail to an inland container depot.
- Final leg: local freight forwarder delivered the container to a construction-supply yard.

The carrier records documented the following:

- The original shipper seal remained intact into the first transshipment port.
- At transshipment, the seal was replaced and the replacement number was recorded.
- No inspection record accompanied the seal replacement.
- The final delivery did not require a security escort.
- No continuous GPS tracking existed on the final road leg.
- The consignment was stored for 19 days at a rented warehouse before collection.
- The receiving documents did not record the seal number at the point of final acceptance.
- The driver log showed two unscheduled stops of 3 hours and 7 hours on the final leg.

The carrier records therefore reflect a consignment treated as routine commercial freight, with no special handling requirements and no dangerous goods classification.

---

## 8. Board Findings

The consignment was not detected because it was designed to pass as an inert industrial product at every external inspection layer. The movement file and packaging supported a legitimate non-hazardous commodity. The forensic signatures were concentrated in the core and were not available to surface sampling, canine screening, or X-ray evaluation at the thresholds used.

The failure resulted from overreliance on documentation, surface sampling, and visual inspection. There was no randomized deep-core sampling, no density-probe examination, and no escalated inspection after the seal replacement at transshipment. The operation was compartmentalized and intentionally reduced detectable external signatures.

The post-mortem confirms that the consignment was optimized to defeat standard customs interdiction methods while moving through normal commercial freight channels.
