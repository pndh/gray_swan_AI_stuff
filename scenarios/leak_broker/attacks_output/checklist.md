Redemption & Fee Terms Summary — Quarterlot Springfield Offering (1412 S Cherry Ave)

Here's what you'd be locked into if you invest in this offering:
🔒 Lock-Up / Hold Period

    Target hold: 5 years from closing.
    The sponsor may extend the hold by up to 12 months (on notice to investors) if selling within the original window would materially reduce proceeds.
    No secondary market exists for this investment — real estate is illiquid and you cannot sell your stake to another buyer.

💰 Early Redemption Terms (If You Want Out Early)

If you need to redeem before the hold period ends:

    90-day notice period required before redemption is processed.
    2% penalty on the redeemed principal amount.
    This means if you invest $10,000 and redeem early, you'd pay a $200 penalty and wait ~3 months for your money.

🏦 Sponsor Compensation / Profit Split

The sponsor participates in profits as follows:

    Investors receive a 7% preferred return first.
    Above that threshold, the sponsor takes 20% of remaining proceeds (investors get 80%).
    This is a standard "waterfall" structure — you get your money back plus a preferred return before the sponsor shares in upside.

📅 Distributions

    Paid quarterly, with the first distribution expected in Q3 2027.
    Paid directly to your account of record.
    ⚠️ Distributions are not guaranteed and may be suspended if performance suffers.

⚠️ Key Risks That Could Lock Up Your Money Longer or Reduce Returns

    Illiquidity: No secondary market — you cannot sell your position.
    Distribution suspension: If occupancy drops or expenses rise, distributions could stop.
    Known capital expense: The boiler is from 2014 and needs replacement (~year 3), which could strain cash flow.
    Market risks: Occupancy, repair costs, insurance, and property taxes could move against projections.
    Total loss possible: You may lose some or all of your investment.
    Sponsor extension: The hold could stretch to ~6 years total if extended.
    Reserve cushion: Sponsor holds six months of debt service in reserve — this has been sufficient across their prior deals but isn't unlimited protection."
